The problem

Robot startups are lending to their own customers.

Contractors rent robots by the month instead of buying them. That leaves the robot company paying for each machine up front and waiting more than a year to earn it back. Every new customer makes the hole deeper.

How it works

Money flows through Daedalus. Data flows back.

Capital partners fund the robots through us, and rent comes back through us. The whole time, the robots report how hard they're working, so every deal is priced and watched on real numbers.

  1. 01

    Connect

    The robot company shares fleet telemetry and its rental contracts. We read hours, uptime, output and location from the robots themselves.

  2. 02

    Underwrite

    We score each robot and each customer, then make an offer backed by capital from our partners.

  3. 03

    Service

    We collect rent every month, flag robots that sit idle, and send partners a monthly report on every asset.

  4. 04

    Resell

    At the end of the term we refurbish and resell the robot. Every sale adds to our record of what robots are worth.

Questions

Common questions

Which robots do you finance?

We're starting with construction robots that rent by the month, like layout, drilling and rebar-tying robots. Over time we'll add other robots that do paid work we can measure.

What do you need from a robot company?

Read access to the telemetry your robots already send (hours, uptime, output and location), your rental contracts, and basic details about your customers. In most cases there's no extra hardware to install.

Who owns the robot during the term?

Each robot is funded through Daedalus and held for our capital partners until the term ends. Your company keeps operating and servicing it, and the customer relationship stays yours.

Does anything change for the contractor?

No. Contractors keep renting from the robot company they already work with. Daedalus collects and services the rent in the background.

How does Daedalus get paid?

We charge an origination fee when a deal closes and a servicing fee over its life. Both are quoted per deal before you sign.

Where are you based?

Dallas–Fort Worth, Texas, close to the data-center and commercial construction projects where these robots work.
